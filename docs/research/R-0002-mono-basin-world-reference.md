## Executive Summary & Simulation Context

This document establishes the ground-truth geospatial, topographic, infrastructure, visual, meteorological, and architectural specification for the primary Tier 1 playable region of **CODEX**: the **Mono Basin**, centered on the rural highway hub of **Lee Vining, California**.

The simulation takes place in present-day **2026**. The narrative journey centers on a grandson who inherits his late grandfather's rural homestead near Lee Vining, accompanied by a 1985–1990 rear-wheel-drive sedan in the barn and a late-1980s work pickup. Driving realism is paramount: all road alignments, grade percentages, surface micro-textures, cross-slopes, and ambient atmospheric pressures directly drive the custom 120 Hz async C++ physics solver (`CodexVehicleDynamics`) and tire slip models (Pacejka MF94).

Per project operating laws, natural features (Mono Lake, Tioga Pass, Mount Dana, Bodie) retain their authentic real names, while settlements, commercial businesses, fuel brands, and specific dwellings receive understated, grounded fictional designations. All geometry is extracted strictly from open, permissive sources (OpenStreetMap under ODbL, USGS 3DEP 1m DEMs in the public domain) to ensure absolute commercial compliance.

## 1. World Box and Areas

### World Bounding Box
The simulation bounding box spans **27.2 km East-West** by **36.1 km North-South**, encompassing approximately **982.0 km²**:
- **West:** `-119.27000°` (UTM 11N Easting: `300,562 m`)
- **East:** `-118.96000°` (UTM 11N Easting: `327,772 m`)
- **South:** `37.89000°` (UTM 11N Northing: `4,195,684 m`)
- **North:** `38.21500°` (UTM 11N Northing: `4,231,788 m`)

### Geographic Scope & Boundary Verification
1. **Tioga Pass & SR 120 West:** The box fully captures the entire 15.6 km eastern ascent of State Route 120 through Lee Vining Canyon, ascending 968 meters past Poole Powerhouse, Warren Fork, Ellery Lake (2,905 m), and Tioga Lake (2,942 m) to the Yosemite National Park Eastern Entrance Station at the Tioga Pass summit (3,033 m / 9,943 ft at `-119.25778°, 37.91083°`).
2. **Mount Dana & High Crest:** Mount Dana's summit (`37.89972°, -119.22111°`, 3,978.2 m NAVD88 / 13,061 ft) lies comfortably inside the southern boundary (`37.89000°`), establishing the maximum vertical boundary of the heightmap.
3. **South Tufa Verification:** **South Tufa IS INSIDE the world box.** Located at `37.94040° N, -119.02720° W`, South Tufa sits 5.60 km north of the southern boundary and 5.89 km west of the eastern boundary.
4. **Bodie State Historic Park & Bodie Road (SR 270):** The ghost town basin (`38.21280° N, -119.01350° W`), the Standard Mill, and the full 20.6 km length of SR 270 (both the 15.8 km paved highway and the 4.8 km severe washboard gravel corridor) lie completely within the box.
5. **Conway Summit (US-395):** The mountain highway pass over the Bodie Hills ridge (`38.08390° N, -119.19140° W`, 2,475 m / 8,143 ft) and its Caltrans maintenance station are centered in the northwestern quadrant.
6. **June Lake Loop Extension (SR 158):** As noted, the June Lake Loop (`37.73° N` to `37.885° N`) sits directly south of the box boundary (`37.89000° N`). It is defined herein as a dedicated `extension` feature for Phase 3 ingestion.

## 2. Roads and Highway Infrastructure

### Road Network Overview
The Mono Basin road network represents a harsh high-desert and subalpine mountain operating theatre. Driving is dominated by three state highways (US-395, SR 120, SR 167), one historical mining artery (SR 270), and remote utility/recreational spurs (Lundy Lake, Poole Power Plant, Test Station).

### Detailed Segment Breakdown (`data/reference/road_facts.csv`)
The 15.6 km climb up Lee Vining Canyon on State Route 120 ascends nearly a vertical kilometer (968 m) with sustained grades between 6% and 10.6%, topping out at a maximum localized pitch of 12.0% on the hanging wall blast bench below Ellery Lake dam. On SR 270, the transition at kilometer 15.82 from smooth asphalt to an unpaved washboard road introduces high-frequency chassis vibration (chatter frequencies of 25–40 Hz at 30 mph across 5–8 cm washboard ridges) that tests suspension damping and tire relaxation lengths.

## 4. Visual Language, Geology and Biomes

### Regional Geology & Surface Character
1. **The Sierran Escarpment & Glacial Canyons:** The western edge of the world is formed by the catastrophic normal fault scarp of the Eastern Sierra. Bedrock is divided between the pale cream/grey Cretaceous Cathedral Peak Granodiorite (featuring giant potassium feldspar phenocrysts up to 5 cm across) and dark, rusty-red Paleozoic metamorphic metavolcanics (hornfels, slate, and quartzite) exposed on Mount Dana and the Tioga Crest.
2. **Volcanic Landforms:** The Mono Basin floor is punctuated by the Mono Craters chain. Panum Crater features pale grey pumice lapilli and dense, obsidian plugs that fracture with razor-sharp conchoidal edges. Black Point on the north shore is an unusual sublacustrine basaltic tuff cone formed underwater during the Pleistocene.
3. **Shoreline & Tufa:** The receding shoreline of Mono Lake exposes wide, blindingly white alkali mudflats coated in efflorescent trona and halite salts. Tufa towers—formed where sublacustrine freshwater springs rich in calcium met carbonate-rich lake brine—rise as porous, cellular, cream-to-tan limestone spires up to 9 meters tall.

### Roadside Details & Infrastructure Props
- **Guardrails:** Caltrans standard galvanized steel W-beam guardrails on galvanized I-beam steel posts along high-speed curves of US-395. Along SR 120 in Lee Vining Canyon, guardrails transition to weathered timber posts with single steel W-beams, occasionally battered and dented by winter snow slides.
- **Avalanche Netting:** Upper Lee Vining Canyon (between Warren Fork and Ellery Lake) features heavy galvanized wire-rope rockfall netting pinned to sheared metamorphic cliffs, alongside concrete catch-ditches filled with fallen gravel.
- **Snow Stakes & Markers:** Steel T-posts and fiberglass snow poles painted fluorescent orange or safety yellow with white reflective bands stand 3 meters tall along all mountain roads, marking culverts and pavement edges for rotary snowplows.
- **Cattle Guards & Fences:** Heavy steel pipe cattle guards with cast-iron grates cross ranch spurs and Bodie Road. Highway right-of-way fences consist of weathered 4-strand barbed wire strung on weathered grey cedar posts and steel T-posts.

### Diurnal Lighting & Atmosphere
- **Dawn & Alpenglow:** At 05:45 in midsummer, the Eastern Sierra crest catches the first direct salmon-pink alpenglow while the basin floor remains in cold blue shadow.
- **Midday Glare:** Under high-elevation sun (UV index 11+), the high-desert landscape appears pale and desaturated. Intense heat shimmer distorts distant views of Paoha Island across the sage flats.
- **Golden Hour Raking Light:** At 18:30 in autumn, low-angle raking sunlight illuminates every texture of the sagebrush shrubs, highlights the orange rust on corrugated tin roofs, and turns the aspen groves in Lundy Canyon into incandescent gold.
- **Night Skies & Darkness:** Mono Basin is an exceptional dark-sky preserve (**Bortle Class 2**, Zenith SQM >= 21.85 mag/arcsec²). The Milky Way core spans from Sagittarius to Cygnus with stunning structural detail, interrupted only by solitary, distant amber sodium-vapor yard lights in Lee Vining.

## 5. Settlements, Architecture and Buildings

### Architectural Character & Town Layout
Lee Vining (settled 1926) is an unpretentious, functional high-desert mountain gateway settlement. Built on a sloping glacial alluvial fan, the town centers entirely along a 900-meter commercial strip of **US-395 (Main Street)**, backed by a compact, four-block residential grid of numbered streets (1st through 4th) and modest cross-avenues (Lee Vining Ave, Mattly Ave, Paoha Drive).

### Construction Typologies
1. **Commercial Motels & Diners:** Single-story 1950s–1970s drive-up motor lodges with exterior concrete walkways, flat gravel-coated roofs, and weathered timber fascias. Siding is painted horizontal stucco or cedar shingle, bleached by intense high-altitude ultraviolet radiation. Diners feature low-pitch gable roofs, plate glass storefront windows, and vintage neon or porcelain-enamel sign fixtures.
2. **Residential High-Desert Dwellings:** Modest timber-framed bungalows and manufactured homes on raised concrete pier foundations. Exterior finishes are dominated by unpainted weathered cedar shingles, horizontal lap siding painted in muted earth tones (oxide red, forest green, weathered cream), and durable corrugated galvanized iron roofing designed to shed heavy wet Sierra spring snow. Propane tanks, firewood cords under blue tarps, and vintage pickup trucks occupy every gravel driveway.
3. **Historic Ghost Town Mining Architecture (Bodie):** Over 100 surviving structures preserved by California State Parks in a state of **arrested decay**. Construction is rough-sawn local pine weatherboards, unpainted and sandblasted into a silvery-charcoal patina by decades of gale-force winds. Roofs are heavily oxidized, orange-rusted corrugated iron. False-front commercial buildings lean gracefully, held by hidden structural steel braces.
4. **Agricultural Outposts & Barns:** Heavy post-and-beam agricultural barns framed from peeled lodgepole and ponderosa pine poles, clad in vertical rough-sawn pine planks and capped with rusted tin roofs. The grandfather's barn is characterized by an oil-stained plank floor, sliding hay doors, an overhead loft, and decades of accumulated tools, spare tires, and agricultural implements.

### Night Illumination & Visual Atmosphere
Lee Vining has no streetlights outside of highway intersection fixtures. Night illumination is sparse, warm, and localized:
- Amber neon signs at the roadside motel and diner.
- Solitary high-pressure sodium and mercury-vapor yard lights mounted on wooden utility poles outside garages and barns, casting soft, circular pools of amber light across gravel yards.
- Warm incandescent 2700K spill through double-hung residential windows.
- Inky, pitch-black horizons beyond the town limits, where asphalt highway surfaces merge into deep sagebrush darkness.

### Critical Weather Phenomena to Model
1. **Summer Afternoon Orographic Thunderstorms (July–August):** Driven by the North American Monsoon, warm moisture surges northward from the Gulf of California. By 13:00, towering cumulus congestus clouds build along the Mount Dana / Tioga Crest ridgeline. Between 14:30 and 17:00, severe localized downpours, dry lightning, and marble-sized graupel (soft hail) strike the high passes, rapidly dropping pavement temperatures from 35°C to 12°C and drastically reducing tire grip (friction coefficient drops from 1.00 to 0.70).
2. **Mono Lake Pre-Frontal Gales & Alkali Dust Storms (Spring & Autumn):** Strong Pacific cold fronts approaching California generate ferocious south-to-southwest downslope pressure gradients. Winds channeling off the Sierra scarp frequently sustain 20–30 m/s (45–65 mph) with localized gusts exceeding 40 m/s (90 mph). Winds sweep across the exposed dry alkaline lakebed, whipping up massive, opaque white dust plumes that reduce visibility on US-395 and SR 167 to less than 50 meters.
3. **Winter Blizzard Cycles & Highway Closures (November–May):** Deep atmospheric river events deposit 1 to 2 meters of heavy, wind-packed snow in single 48-hour storm cycles along SR 120. Snow drifts across the Lee Vining Canyon hairpins reach 4 meters in depth, triggering massive wet-slab avalanches that enforce total winter closure of Tioga Pass until rotary plows clear the corridor in late spring.
4. **Winter Temperature Inversions & Lake Fog ("Pogonip"):** Under calm winter high pressure, cold, dense air pools in the Mono Basin depression beneath warmer air aloft. Dense radiation fog forms over the hypersaline water. In sub-freezing temperatures, this moisture supercools into **pogonip** (frozen fog), coating sagebrush, power lines, and fence wires in thick, delicate needles of white rime ice.

## 7. Fiction Mapping and Regional Adaptation

### Fictionalization Principles
To maintain profound emotional resonance while honoring legal integrity:
1. **Natural Geography is Real:** Mono Lake, Tioga Pass, Mount Dana, Bodie, Lundy Canyon, and all numbered state/federal highways (US-395, SR 120, SR 167, SR 270) retain their exact real-world names.
2. **Settlements and Businesses are Fictional:** Towns, service stations, motels, diners, general stores, and personal residences are given understated, authentic, historically plausible names that reflect local geography, pioneer families, and geology.
3. **Zero Trademark Infringement:** No commercial oil companies (Chevron, Mobil, Shell) or trademarked hospitality chains exist.
4. **No Novelty Puns:** Names avoid sarcastic or exaggerated puns, preserving the serious, quiet, warm, and bittersweet tone of the narrative.
